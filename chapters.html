Chapter I

The Founder

The Age of Survival

“Every civilization begins with someone willing to build where nothing yet exists.”
Chapter Progress 0 of 0 complete

Theme

Survival · Self-Sufficiency · Hope

Your founders arrive in an untamed land with little more than determination and the hope of building something that will outlive them. There are no neighbors, institutions, or public spaces. There is only a small home, open land, and the responsibility of creating a future where none yet exists.

Everything that follows throughout the next nineteen chapters depends upon the choices made here.

The Question

Can one family create a foundation strong enough for a civilization to grow?

During this chapter, the entire civilization consists of one household. Every meal must be earned, every improvement must be built, and every relationship outside the Dynasty must be sought beyond its borders.

Visual Identity

A humble beginning

Begin in a Tier 1 Micro Home. The architecture may resemble a tiny farmhouse, cabin, cottage, or practical homestead. Use simple interiors, natural materials, limited decoration, and clothing suited to work rather than luxury. Brown, green, cream, and white are suggested, but the story matters more than strict colors.

Chapter Rules

Survive through your own labor

No traditional careers

Neither founder may join a standard career during this chapter. Income must come through the work of the household.

Acceptable income

Gardening, livestock, fishing, foraging, cross stitch, woodworking, nectar making, crafted goods, and collectibles are permitted.

Foreign trade

The civilization does not yet have a marketplace. When practical, travel to another world to sell goods. These worlds represent established neighboring civilizations.

The homestead

The first home should grow naturally alongside the Dynasty. Necessity comes before luxury.

Legacy Left Behind

The Founder proves civilization can exist.

At the end of Chapter I, the Dynasty should leave behind its first home, a thriving homestead, a stable source of food, a child prepared to continue the work, and a vision for the civilization still to come.

Completing this chapter prepares the civilization for Chapter II: The Age of Community, when new households may begin arriving and the first institution can be constructed.
